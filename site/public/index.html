The art

More than 1,300 species, each painted by hand.

In the 1800s, a handful of naturalists set out to paint every bird they could find, and to print each picture by hand. John James Audubon’s The Birds of America and John Gould’s books of Europe, Australia, Asia and Great Britain are among the most beautiful books ever made. In 2010, a single copy of The Birds of America sold for $11.5 million.

Featherframe shows one illustration at a time, with the species’ name, its Latin name and the book’s own caption set beneath it.

How it works

Meet the birds you only hear.

Most birds nearby are heard long before they are seen. Featherframe puts a name and a portrait to each call, so over a season you come to know who lives around you.

BirdWeather is a public map of listening stations in gardens and parks around the world. Each one uses BirdNET, software from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and Chemnitz University of Technology, to name the birds it hears. Choose a station near you, or near somewhere you love: a parent’s garden, or the coast you visit every summer. When it names a species, the frame shows it. You can also add a station of your own.

The collage

Each night, a portrait of the day.

The frame shows every species heard that day on one sheet, numbered and keyed like a page in an old natural history book.

Turn on AI illustration and the day’s species are painted together in one scene, on your own OpenAI account. It is off until you turn it on, and everything it paints carries a ✦. Without it, the day is laid out in the original illustrations.

✦ Each collage was painted by AI from the species heard that day at one listening station.

Questions
Does it make a good gift?
Yes. Nothing needs setting up before you wrap it. The person you give it to connects it to their Wi-Fi and chooses a listening station, all from their phone. It ships in time for the holidays.
Do I need Wi-Fi?
Yes. The frame uses your Wi-Fi to receive each new illustration, and needs nothing else after setup.
Do I need to run BirdNET locally?
No. The frame can use any public BirdWeather station, and there are stations across North America, Europe and beyond. If none is close, choose one near a place you love. To see the birds in your own yard, you can add a station of your own.
Does it work outside North America?
Yes. Gould’s books cover the birds of Europe, Asia and Australia, and the frame picks the book that illustrated your species.
What if a species near me was never illustrated?
The frame shows its name, set in the type of the old books. With AI illustration turned on, it paints one in the books’ style instead, marked ✦.
Do the collages need AI?
No. Without AI, the day is laid out in the original illustrations, numbered and keyed like a page in an old natural history book. With AI illustration turned on, the day’s species are painted together in one scene, marked ✦.
What is hosting, and what does it cost?
Hosting picks each illustration and sends it to your frame. It is included with every frame, and there is no subscription.
Is my data private?
The frame has no camera and no microphone. Your account holds your frame’s settings and the species it has shown, and nothing is sold or shared.
