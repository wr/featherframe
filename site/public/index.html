The art

More than 1,300 species, each painted by hand.

In the 1800s, a handful of naturalists set out to paint every bird they could find, and to print each picture by hand. John James Audubon’s The Birds of America and John Gould’s books of Europe, Australia, Asia and Great Britain are among the most beautiful books ever made. In 2010, a single copy of The Birds of America sold for $11.5 million.

Featherframe shows one illustration at a time, with the species’ name, its Latin name and the book’s own caption set beneath it.

How it works

Meet the birds you only hear.

Most birds nearby are heard long before they are seen. Featherframe puts a name and a portrait to each call, so over a season you come to know who lives around you.

BirdNET was created by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and Chemnitz University of Technology to identify birdsong in the name of wildlife conservation and research. It’s a small, freely available AI model that can run on hobbyist single-board computers like Raspberry Pi with a simple microphone plugged in.

You can set up a BirdNET detector in your own backyard, or choose a BirdWeather station near you or anywhere in the world.

The collage

Each night, a portrait of the day.

During quiet hours overnight, your frame shows every species heard that day on one canvas, numbered and labeled like a page in an old natural history book.

Enable AI illustration¹ and all the day’s species are painted together as a beautiful collage that changes with the seasons and weather.

✦ Each collage was painted by AI from the species heard that day by one detection source.

¹ Requires your own OpenAI API key, billed separately by OpenAI. Featherframe and Wells Workshop LLC are not affiliated with OpenAI. Use of AI is not required for collage functionality.

Questions
Does it make a good gift?
Yes. Nothing needs setting up before you wrap it. The person you give it to connects it to their Wi-Fi and chooses a detection source, all from their phone. US pre-orders ship by December 12.
Do I need Wi-Fi?
Yes. The frame uses your Wi-Fi to receive each new illustration, and needs nothing else after setup.
How fast does the frame change?
  • Grayscale: about a second.
  • Color: about fifteen seconds, flickering as its inks settle. Well suited to the nightly collage, and fine for detections.
Do I need to run BirdNET locally?
No. The frame can use any public BirdWeather station, and there are stations across North America, Europe and beyond. If none is close, choose one near a place you love. To see the birds in your own yard, you can add a station of your own.
Does it work outside North America?
Yes. Gould’s books cover the birds of Europe, Asia and Australia, and the frame picks the book that illustrated your species.
What if a species near me was never illustrated?
The frame shows its name, set in the type of the old books. With AI illustration¹ turned on, it paints one in the books’ style instead, marked ✦.
Do the collages need AI?
No. Without AI, the day is laid out in the original illustrations, numbered and keyed like a page in an old natural history book. With AI illustration¹ turned on, the day’s species are painted together in one scene, marked ✦.
Is a subscription required?
No. The frame is a one-time purchase, and BirdNET is free, open-source software offered by a third party. The Featherframe webapp (used to connect to BirdNET or BirdWeather and send pictures to your frame) is also free and open source, and a hosted version of the webapp is provided free to purchasers of the frame.
Is hosting required?
  • If you use Featherframe’s hosted webapp, yes.
  • If you run the webapp yourself (on your BirdNET device, in Docker and so on), no.
Is my data private?
The frame has no camera and no microphone. Your account holds your frame’s settings and the species it has shown, and nothing is sold or shared.
